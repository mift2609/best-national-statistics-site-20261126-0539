Land & sea: an archipelago on the equator

Indonesia straddles the equator for more than 5,000 kilometres — wide enough that when Jakarta sits down to lunch, Papua is already mid-afternoon. These are the measures of the place itself.

One country, many peoples

The 2010 census classified 1,340 ethnic groups across the archipelago, and the language agency counts 718 living local languages alongside the national language, Indonesian. That variety is also an attraction: 13.9 million international visitors arrived in 2024.
